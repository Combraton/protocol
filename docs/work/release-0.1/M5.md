## Outcome

Two profiles that any provider can implement and any reader can check:
- `knowledge/1`: claims with immutable revisions and declared ancestry, three semantic planes, structural conflict and drift records, applicability evaluations against a target basis, reliance decisions under one explicit authority binding per scope, and history with explicit links.
- `verification/1`: exact contracts, `evaluate_contract` job references, immutable receipts mapped to sealed artifacts, and a read-only assessment in which missing, unavailable or outdated verification is never a pass.

Two cross-profile additions: packets that carry claims, enforced at required boundaries; and a provider that reconnects with a changed capability.

Conformance again uses **reference participants only**: a reference knowledge provider, a reference verifier with a scripted evaluator, and the M4 context, evidence and execution references. **M5 does not build the real CBR memory engine or PIO adapters**, and it does not implement Coordination.

## What M5 will establish, and what it will not

**Establishes**, once accepted with fixtures, mutants and independent evidence:
- Evidence, claims, decisions, evaluations, conflicts and receipts are separate records with separate identities; no claim or receipt records a decision.
- A claim revision never changes. Revisions, corrections, supersession and conflicts are later records with explicit links, and history keeps all of them.
- Reliance, applicability, subject health and evidence availability are four facets, each from its own records.
- Declared ancestry is classified deterministically. Shared, partial or unknown ancestry never produces corroboration, and nothing is called independent.
- A conflict needs a structural incompatibility. Disjoint qualifiers, basis or validity, and multi-valued predicates are not conflicts, and a potential conflict is never presented as demonstrated.
- Only the one bound authority for a scope decides reliance or resolves conflicts. It may adopt its own claim, and that adoption is recorded as such. Grants, derivation labels, producers and verifiers never decide.
- Applicability follows a fixed precedence. An observed mismatch is `invalid_for_target`, incomplete checks are never `applicable`, and `needs_check` and `unknown` are never shown as stale.
- A receipt names exact subjects for every contract role, the exact contract, evaluator, environment and a result for every property, and maps to one sealed artifact.
- An assessment states its time and basis. Mismatched, unverifiable, stale, before-observation, not evaluated and indeterminate are never satisfied, and a caller-selected time never establishes present validity.
- A packet carrying claims keeps immutable snapshots. A required binding whose claim loses its permitted use or becomes invalid is blocked at its boundary, unavailable knowledge is unknown, advisory work proceeds, and nothing is substituted.
- Work pinned to an evaluator keeps its meaning when a provider reconnects with that evaluator changed.

**Does not establish:**
- that any claim is true, that declared ancestry is honest, or that an evaluator is correct;
- semantic equivalence or incompatibility beyond structural comparison;
- memory quality, retrieval quality or context usefulness; those belong to CBR's own evaluation plans;
- project acceptance or gate evaluation; Coordination is unsupported in 0.1, and the local authority binding is the standalone mode;
- temporal reconstruction views, which are deferred (M5-Q8);
- signed receipts or offline verification, deferred with Remote trust (U2);
- mixed-implementation composition: M5 compositions still run one implementation for every participant (see [carried-forward coverage](#m4-coverage-carried-forward)).

## Intended behavior in examples

Identifiers are illustrative.

**1. Drift is not a contradiction; different targets are not conflicts (KNW-2, KNW-5).**
- **Drift.** Scope `svc`, subject `{ kind: "service", id: "billing" }`, predicate `queue`, `cardinality: single`. Revision A is `normative`, value `"v2"`, accepted for `binding` use. Revision B is `observed`, value `"v1"`, basis tree `t9`.
  - Opening a record on A and B gives kind **drift**.
  - A has no basis, so the dimension `basis` is uncertain, and the status is `potential`.
  - Both revisions keep their records and facets.
- **Disjoint qualifiers.** "workers = 5" with qualifiers `{ environment: "staging" }`, against "workers = 3" with `{ environment: "production" }`, is refused: `claims_not_comparable`, reason `qualifiers_disjoint`.
- **Multiple values.** "language = English" and "language = French" with `cardinality: multiple` are refused with `multiple_values_permitted`.
- **Demonstrated.** Two `observed` revisions are `demonstrated` only with:
  - equal qualifiers;
  - equal complete bases;
  - fully known, overlapping validity;
  - `single` cardinality.

**2. Declared ancestry, classified deterministically (KNW-4).**
- **Shared root.** Three summaries repeat "the auth flow is correct", each declaring complete root `{ kind: "evidence", provider: "cbr-1", artifact: "derivation.d1", digest: … }`. The class is `single_lineage`, however often it repeats.
- **Pairwise overlap.** Roots `{A,B}`, `{B,C}` and `{A,C}`, all complete, give `overlapping_lineages`: every pair shares an origin, and none is common to all.
- **Disjoint.** A complete `{A}` and a complete `{D}`, where `D` is a separately captured CI report, give `multiple_lineages`. That still does not say the sources are independent.
- **Unknown ancestry.** A wrapper artifact with `completeness: unknown` adds nothing. `{A}` plus that entry is `undetermined`, never `multiple_lineages`.
- **Equal digests.** Two roots with different references but equal digests are never disjoint.

**3. A model cannot pick a winner (KNW-5).**
- The CBR maintenance principal opens a record on two `interpretive` revisions about a retry limit, with a suggested test in `note`.
- Its `conflict.resolve` is `not_authority`. A newer revision from the same producer resolves nothing.
- The bound authority resolves with `narrow_scope`, and both revisions and the resolution stay in history.

**4. A human states and adopts a requirement; a model cannot (KNW-6).**
- The bound authority `owner` proposes "billing must use queue v2", then records `accepted_for_use` for `binding` use. The decision records `author_is_decider: true`, presented as adoption by its author, not review.
- Model producer `cbr-model` proposes a `normative` claim and labels its derivation `human`. Its own `decision.record` is `not_authority`, because it is not the bound authority, whatever the label says.

**5. Four facets at once (KNW-3).**

```json
{ "reference": { "provider": "cbr-1", "claim": "parser-handles-bom", "revision": 2, "digest": "sha256:…" },
  "reliance": { "state": "accepted_for_use", "permitted_use": "evidence", "decision": "dec-7", "author_is_decider": false },
  "applicability": [ { "evaluation": "ev-3", "target": { "repositories": [ { "id": "repo-a", "tree": "t12" } ], "completeness": "complete" }, "result": "invalid_for_target", "evaluator": { "id": "tree", "version": "1" } } ],
  "health": "not_applicable",
  "availability": { "state": "purged", "counts": { "available": 0, "unavailable": 0, "purged": 1, "unknown": 0 } } }
```

The claim was accepted at `t10`. At `t12` the tree named by its condition differs, and its supporting test log was purged. None of these facts rewrites the others.

**6. Applicability precedence (KNW-7).** A claim has condition `k1` (tree of `repo-a`), condition `k2` (`environment_digest`, which this evaluator does not support), and dependency `D@1`.
- Target tree differs for `k1`: `invalid_for_target`, even though `k2` is unsupported.
- Tree matches, target lacks `environment`: `unknown`.
- Anchors all present and matching, but `D@1` has no evaluation for this target: `needs_check`.
- Everything matches and `D@1` is `applicable` for this target: `applicable`.

In a packet, the `needs_check` and `unknown` cases are not `stale` and not historical.

**7. An extracted instruction stays a proposal (KNW-9).** A model extracts "always run migrations before tests" as a `normative` claim, which stays `proposed`. A packet can carry it only as a `hypothesis` or `inferred` section. A `binding` item it would satisfy is `unmet` with reason `not_accepted` until the bound authority accepts it for `binding` use. The earlier packet is never rewritten.

**8. Assessment states its basis and time (VER-1, VER-4).** Contract `c1` has roles `tree` and `output` and required properties `parser.unit` and `upload.journey`. The receipt reports `parser.unit: pass` and `upload.journey: indeterminate` (`browser_unavailable`).
- Now, with both roles matching: `parser.unit` satisfied, `upload.journey` not satisfied, `overall: not_satisfied`, `time_basis: provider_clock`.
- Omitting role `output` from the request: `invalid_envelope`.
- A contract reference at another artifact with an equal digest: `contract_mismatch`.
- The contract artifact purged: `contract: unverifiable` (`contract_unavailable`), no property satisfied.
- `at` set to last week, after `observed_until`: at most `satisfied` for that instant, with `present_validity: not_established`.
- `at` before `observed_until`: `before_observation`.

**9. Scoped observations (VER-2, VER-5).** A receipt reports `fail` for execution `e-5`'s output, and `e-5` keeps every axis. A fully satisfied receipt for claim `parser-handles-bom` leaves reliance `proposed`. The verifier's `decision.record` is `not_authority`, and the bound authority may accept the claim and cite the receipt.

**10. Reconnect with a changed evaluator (SCN-7).** Job `j1` pins `journey` version 1 and records `upload.journey: pass`. The verifier restarts with version 1 removed and version 2 installed; the caller reconnects and sees `core.capabilities.changed`.
- `j1` completes: `upload.journey: pass` stays, and its unfinished `upload.visual` is `indeterminate` (`evaluator_unavailable`).
- A new job pinning version 1 is `capability_unavailable`; one pinning version 2 runs.
- `j1`'s receipt names version 1.

**11. Claims in packets, enforced at the boundary (KNW-10, SCN-16).**
- Request `r` has a required `binding` item `i-req` and an advisory item `i-adv`, each `claim_included`. Packet `r@1` (`combraton-context-packet/2`) carries claim `A@1` for `i-req` and `B@1` for `i-adv`, both accepted for `binding` use and `applicable` to the basis.
- Execution `e-req` binds `r@1` `required_before_start`, and `e-adv` binds it `advisory`, both under `execution.claim_revalidation`.
- **Rejection.** The authority rejects `A@1`. `context.packet.inspect` reports:
  - `claim_changes: [ { section_id: "s-a", claim: A@1, change: "reliance_changed" } ]`;
  - `invalidated_items: [ { item_id: "i-req", claim: A@1, reason: "permitted_use_lost" } ]`.

  `e-req`'s `packet.facts` is `mismatch` (`claim invalidated: i-req`), so it is not dispatched (`context_binding_stale`), while `e-adv` proceeds.
- **Outage.** If instead the knowledge provider is unreachable, `unverified_items` names `i-req` (`knowledge_unavailable`), and `e-req` waits as `context_binding_unknown`.
- **Newer revision.** A newer revision `A@2` is reported `lineage_revised` and is never substituted.

## Provider and consumer assumptions, with counterexamples

| Assumption | Counterexample the contract must survive |
|---|---|
| A reader can tell which revision a statement came from | A packet cites "latest": after revise, the same citation means different content. Hence exact revision references with recomputable digests. |
| Different targets are not contradictions | Staging and production settings, two supported languages, or trees `t1` and `t2` flagged as conflicts. Hence structural comparison with refusal reasons, and potential versus demonstrated. |
| An authority is identifiable per scope | Two standalone tools both configured to accept for `svc`. Hence one binding per scope, transfer by epoch. |
| Permission comes from authentication, not labels | A model labels its derivation `human` to accept its output. Hence the binding decides, and labels are descriptive. |
| Support can be compared | The same derivation wrapped in three artifacts with unknown ancestry. Hence declared, provider-qualified roots, and `undetermined` rather than forced classes. |
| Applicability is decided against a target | An accepted claim assumed valid on a new branch, or an unevaluated claim shown as stale. Hence explicit precedence, and `needs_check` or `unknown` kept distinct. |
| A receipt is about exact subjects and contract | A passing receipt for `t12` reused for `t13`, or a different contract with an equal digest. Hence every role compared and exact contract references. |
| An assessment is about a stated moment | A caller asks about last week and presents the answer as current. Hence `time_basis` and `present_validity`. |
| Unavailable is not a failure and not a pass | A browser outage recorded as `fail` stops unrelated work, or recorded as `pass` hides a missing journey. Hence `indeterminate`, `not_satisfied`, and no stop semantics. |
| Capability meaning is pinned | An evaluator upgraded mid-job appears in old receipts. Hence pinned evaluator versions and durable per-property results. |
| Readers verify what they read | A knowledge provider serves an altered claim record under its old digest. Hence recomputed digests, as the M4 executor checks packet bytes. |

**Changes from the proposals:**
- **Codes and reasons:** the proposed `authority_scope_bound` code and `self_decision` reason are not used. A scope's binding is the subject whose ID is the scope, so a second binding fails its precondition, and self-adoption is recorded instead of refused.
- **Evaluator predicates** name each version (`verification.evaluator.<id>.<version>`), so a version change is a capability change without altering Core's predicate shape.
- **Execution** gains the negotiated `execution.claim_revalidation` for boundary enforcement.

## Acceptance

None may be dropped silently. If one cannot be met, it is escalated with evidence and recorded as deferred.

1. **Separate records.** Evidence, claim revisions, decisions, evaluations, conflicts and receipts have separate identities. No operation on one records another implicitly.
2. **Revisions and history.** Immutable revisions with base checks and recomputable digests; later records with explicit links; unknown validity stays unknown.
3. **Independent facets.** Reliance, applicability, health and availability coexist in any combination.
4. **Declared ancestry.** The classification is deterministic, including partial overlap and unknown ancestry; no wrapper or equal digest manufactures corroboration.
5. **Structural conflicts.** Disjoint qualifiers, basis or validity, multi-valued predicates and equal values are refused with their reasons; potential is distinct from demonstrated.
6. **Authority.** Only the bound authority decides or resolves. Self-adoption is recorded, and grants, labels, producers and verifiers are refused.
7. **Applicability.** The precedence is enforced; incomplete checks never yield `applicable`; `needs_check` and `unknown` are never stale.
8. **Complete receipts.** Every role and property is listed; the receipt maps to one sealed artifact.
9. **No pass by absence or age.** The assessment's matching rules, time basis and `present_validity` behave as specified.
10. **Scoped observations.** A failing receipt changes no execution state; a passing receipt grants no authority.
11. **Packets carrying claims.** Snapshots are immutable and changes reported; required bindings are blocked at their boundary when a claim loses its use, becomes invalid or is unverifiable; advisory work proceeds; no substitution; negotiated compatibility.
12. **Pinned capability meaning.** A reconnecting provider with a changed evaluator neither switches versions, rewrites results, nor reports pass after loss.
13. **Reference participants only**, with coverage limits recorded and same-implementation compositions never described as mixed-implementation proof.
14. **Independent evidence** for what the independent implementation claims, with divergences resolved.
15. **CI** green with manifests, transcripts and mutant summaries, and outcomes kept distinct.
